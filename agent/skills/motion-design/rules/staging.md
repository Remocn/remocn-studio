# Staging

Plan what the viewer should notice before scheduling entrances. State the message
and dominant subject for each beat. A frame may have one focal element; additional
objects support the explanation or visual world.

## Keyframes

Inspect the starting state, central action/transformation and result before the
full timeline. Compare crop, scale, type, contrast and material with the primary
reference. Make important UI readable at normal viewing size. Inspect actual assets
instead of substituting generic cards that only describe the promised result.

## Group by meaning

A card and its contents can enter as one unit. Separate arrivals help when the
viewer should read elements in sequence. Choose stagger by logical grouping and
reading order; simultaneous entries are valid for one object or a coordinated event.
A long cascade should earn its screen time through the information it reveals.

An origin can communicate cause or containment: a panel opens from its trigger,
a detail expands from its parent. A fade or direct cut is also valid when no spatial
origin needs explaining. Choose scale range from the gesture rather than applying
a universal minimum or compulsory growth to every fade.

## Product actions

Keep target, cursor arrival, activation, response and result on a shared timeline.
Make the interaction point visible before it matters. Choose camera targets from
actual UI geometry. Retain enough context to identify a detail; an isolated macro
can work after that context is established.

Order a click as cursor arrival < press ≤ first visible response, with the cursor
resting on the target for at least a frame; dead frames after the press read as
latency. A montage may cut from cursor-on-button straight to the result. While a
cursor drags, compute the object and any property the gesture drives (a slider
fill, a reveal width) from the cursor position plus its grab offset, not from a
curve of its own; check that the offset is constant on every drag frame. A sticker
meant to trail the cursor is the exception. Change content at rest, not during fast
travel, unless the motion is meant to hide the swap: the swap frame should fall
where the per-frame deltas are near zero.

For a morph, place the starting object at the geometry needed for the handoff.
Avoid an unexplained font-size or padding change immediately before the morph.
Derive the next object's anchor from the first instead of maintaining unrelated
position constants.

## Expression

Anticipation and follow-through can convey a physical or playful action. Use them
where that meaning belongs; a cursor response or rigid panel may work directly.
Focus and readability decide how many simultaneous gestures the frame can carry.
